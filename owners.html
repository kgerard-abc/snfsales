      Money paid to companies the business owners also control, by home

      Every nursing home that takes Medicare files a yearly cost report with the federal government. One part of it, Worksheet A-8-1, lists what the home paid to "related parties": companies the home's own owners also control, such as the landlord it pays rent to, a management company, or a therapy or staffing company. This table shows those payments for each Rhode Island home, from its newest cost report on file. The reports come from CMS's Healthcare Cost Report Information System (HCRIS), form CMS-2540-10; the public files run about two years behind.

      How to read this table
      • One row per home, its newest report. Click the home's name to see every year it has filed, the related companies named in the reports, and its ratings and inspections.
      • Dollars and shares. Each dollar figure is what the home paid that year; the small percentage under it is that amount as a share of the home's net patient revenue. "All related-party" is the total; "Rent" and "Mgmt fees" (management fees) are the two biggest parts of it.
      • Operator that year is who ran the home in the year of that report: the buyer in the newest sale on this page approved by the end of that year, or the chain or owner CMS lists if the home never changed hands. If the home was sold after that report, the sale is noted under the name, so money paid before a sale is not shown under the buyer's name.
      • Part of a year means the report covers less than 11 months, usually because the home changed hands mid-year; its margin and shares are not comparable with a full year.
      • Sold is the year of the home's first sale on this page. Before sale → newest compares all related-party payments as a share of revenue in the year before that sale with the newest report.
      • Landlord = operator? asks whether the people who own the home's operator also own its building. Yes: the cost report itself shows rent to a related landlord. Likely: the state application was filed as a pair of companies, one to run the home and one to buy the building, by the same applicant, but the newest cost report predates the sale, so it does not show the rent yet. No related rent: no related-party rent in the cost report, so the home either owns its building or rents from an unrelated landlord; the report alone cannot tell which. Partly, No, and Unknown come from the state application. The answer is about whoever runs the home now. When a sale is pending, the buyer's plan is shown under it as "Pending sale," since the application describes what would happen, not who owns the building today.
      • Why it matters. Rent and fees paid to companies the business owners also control are costs to the home, so a home can report a loss while its owners are paid through the landlord or management company. A share that rises after a sale means more of the home's revenue is leaving it this way.
