To the Health Services Council:

On behalf of the Office of the Health Care Advocate at the Rhode Island Office of the Attorney
General (the “Office”), we submit this letter in response to Change in Effective Control Applications
(CEC Application) for Greenwood Center, a 130-bed skilled nursing facility in Warwick, RI.

This Office has commented previously on the use of related entities in skilled nursing transactions,
both generally and as to prior transactions involving Mayflower Healthcare LLC. No related entities
have been disclosed in the present transaction, but the application indicates that Greenwood
Operations RI LLC may exercise an option to purchase Greenwood RI Propco, LLC, which could
result in these entities coming under common ownership at some point in the future.

Recognizing the special circumstances presented by the bankruptcy proceedings, this Office is
nevertheless concerned about future use of related entities, which can have a significant impact on the
long-term financial health of skilled nursing facilities in Rhode Island. We recommend that the Health
Services Council conduct further inquiry and consider conditioning future approvals on updated
disclosures should the purchase option with respect to Greenwood RI Propco, LLC be exercised and
the entities come under common ownership.

Background

Related entities are companies under common ownership with a nursing home administrator with
whom the nursing home contracts. The risk to the nursing home is that if related entities are charging
above-market rates for the contracted services, the precious resources of these facilities will be diverted
away from patient care as profit. Transactions involving related entities may be linked to asset shielding

(where single-use LLCs serve to insulate parent companies from liability) and profit tunnelling (where
LLCs under common ownership migrate profits away from patient care through inflated fees). The
use of related entities may also lead to distorted financial reporting to Medicare and Medicaid,
permitting nursing home operators to list expenses as “costs” billed through arms-length transactions,
when they are in fact profits shifted to common owners.

A working paper published by the National Bureau of Economic Research suggests 68% of profits in
the skilled nursing care industry were hidden through related entity transactions in 2019. 1 Price
increases were especially prevalent in real estate and management services, with costs increasing by
42.4% and 24.6%, respectively, when the operator transacted with a related entity.

Related Entities in this Transaction

This transaction includes a CEC application for the operating entity (indirectly owned by Mayflower
Healthcare LLC and ESRI Holdco LLC) and a CEC application for the real estate entity (owned by
Jacob Taub and Menachem Kofman). To ensure a diligent review of the application and to guard
against potential risks to patient care, the Office recommends that the Health Services Council review
for and address the following issues raised by the application:

    1. Does Greenwood Operations RI LLC Intend to Exercise its Purchase Option?

       The application states that there are no business relationships between Mayflower Healthcare
       LLC, ESRI Holdco LLC, and the operating entities (see CEC Application for Greenwood
       Operations RI LLC at 25, Appendix G, Question 3)—but also indicates that Greenwood
       Operations RI LLC (via these same owners) retains an option to purchase the real estate
       entities that are currently under separate ownership. This would put real estate and operations
       under common ownership and will likely result in them entering a lease arrangement. The
       Office recommends that if Greenwood Operations RI LLC exercises this option, the Health
       Services Council should require the applicant to resubmit responses to all questions included
       as part of the present CEC application, including budget projections, to ensure that the Health
       Services Council has a complete picture of how the addition of related entities will impact the
       financial health of the facility.

    2. Does this Transaction involve Consulting Agreements Between Related Entities?

       In April 2025, the Health Services Council approved a transaction involving Mayflower
       Healthcare LLC (three SNFs, operating entities only) that included significant related entities
       issues. Specifically, the transaction disclosed a consulting agreement with RGW Consulting,
       LLC, but did not disclose that the entity was commonly owned by Zev Schwartz. 2

   at 6, 25. The Office recommends that the Health Services Council inquire as to whether

3. Will This Transaction Result in Payroll Reductions for RNs, LPs, and Nursing Aides?

   According to the payroll expense sheet, Greenwood Center proposes to increase staffing
   without increasing payroll and fringe benefits. The Office is concerned that any cost-cutting
   measures create a risk of potentially underpaying and/or undercompensating staff; this risk is
   illustrated below by comparing FY2024 to FY2025, which shows a net increase in FTEs
   without a commensurate increase in payroll/fringe benefits. This results in a per capita
   reduction in payroll and fringe benefits, which is anticipated to persist through FY2028.

   The Office recommends that the Health Services Council inquire into the impact of these
   reductions, whether they represent true pay cuts and/or reduction in benefits for nursing staff,
   and if so, how the applicant plans to mitigate anticipated impacts on patient care.

4. How will the Proposed Rent Increase Impact Patient Care?
   According to the profit and loss sheet, Greenwood Center proposes to increase the rent by
   95% between FY2025 and FY2026, with an additional 5.5% increase planned in 2028. The
   Office asks the Health Services Council to inquire as to the likely impact of a 95% rent increase
   and suggests that the applicant describe how it will mitigate likely impacts on patient care.
   This Office strongly recommends that the Health Services Council inquire into the reasons
   for this significant increase in lease amount and request documentation demonstrating that
   this amount represents fair market value.

        According to the profit and loss sheet, Greenwood Center includes a line item for “patient

Conclusion

The Office recommends that the Health Services Council make a full inquiry as to these and any other
relevant terms prior to approval of this CEC application. We hope that future reviews can incorporate
a standing review of related entities held by the applicant and any plans to utilize related entities in the
administration of Rhode Island facilities following CEC approval.

Please let us know if there are any questions we can answer to assist in your review.
