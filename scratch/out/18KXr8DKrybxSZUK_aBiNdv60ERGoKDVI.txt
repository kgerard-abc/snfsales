I. SYNOPSIS

The Health Services Council (“Council”) recommends that the applications Coventry
Operations RI LLC [Mayflower Healthcare LLC - sole member Zev Schwartz] for a
Change in Effective Control of Respiratory and Rehab Center of Rhode Island [LTC00789],
a 210-bed skilled nursing facility located at 10 Woodland Drive, Coventry, Rhode Island;
Greenville Operations RI LLC [Mayflower Healthcare LLC - sole member Zev
Schwartz] for a Change in Effective Control of Greenville Skilled Nursing and
Rehabilitation [LTC00792], a 131-bed skilled nursing facility located at 735 Putnam Pike,
Greenville, Rhode Island; and Warren Operations RI LLC [Mayflower Healthcare LLC
- sole member Zev Schwartz] for a Change in Effective Control of Warren Skilled Nursing
and Rehabilitation [LTC00796], a 63-bed skilled nursing facility located at 642 Metacom
Avenue, Warren, Rhode Island be approved.


II. PROPOSAL DESCRIPTION

As presented in the applications, Coventry Operations RI LLC (“Coventry Operations”),
Greenville Operations RI LLC (“Greenville Operations”), and Warren Operations RI LLC
(“Warren Operations”) (collectively the “Applicants”) seek Change in Effective Control
(“CEC”) approvals to acquire Respiratory and Rehab Center of Rhode Island (“Respiratory”),
Greenville Skilled Nursing and Rehabilitation (“Greenville”), and Warren Skilled Nursing and
Rehabilitation (“Warren”), respectively.

III.   INTRODUCTION

"Licensing of Health Care Facilities," the Applicants filed three CEC applications of the
subject-licensed facilities. These requests are made because the statute requires that any
proposed change in owner, operator or lessee of a licensed health care facility be reviewed by
the Council and approved by the state-licensing agency prior to implementation.

Katie Gradowski: “… I'm here on behalf of the Office of the Health Advocate of the Rhode
Island Attorney General's office. Our office submitted comment in response to the
applications by Mayflower Healthcare LLC regarding the acquisition of these facilities
and the transition of operation and our letter addressed concerns regarding related
entities.

So, what is a related entity? A related entity is an entity that is under common ownership
with the -- with whom the operator contracts. So, for instance, real estate holding
companies, management companies, pharmacies, medical supply companies….

So why does this matter? What is our concern? Based on our review of the literature, we
have really three areas of concern and our primary area of concern is the risk of tunneling
profits. There is a growing body of literature that suggests that related entities can be used
to shift profits, to tunnel profits away from the operators effectively moving assets out of
the primary firm to the related entity through inflated pricing. In our comment letter, we
cited a working paper from the National Bureau of Economic Research, which looked at
the pricing for related entities in the context of real estate and consulting services and
those authors found that contracting with a related ·entity as opposed to a true third party
actually drove up pricing substantially. They found that for real estate services,
contracting with a related entity increased their reported cost by 42.4 percent. They found
that for consulting services contracting with related entity as opposed to a true third party
increased the cost by 24.6 percent, and they actually estimated those costs in terms of
markups per bed across the period…

We are concerned with indemnification provisions that have the potential to leave
operators liable for claims against the consultants and their subcontractors.· We are
concerned about provisions that appear to grant a security interest in the assets of the
operators, and we are concerned broadly with creation of an independent contractor
relationship between RGW Consulting and Mayflower, which are -- when those entities
are wholly owned and operated by the same person.· And when I refer to Mayflower, I'm
referring to it as the owner of the three applicants who are before you
We are concerned that these terms effectively permit RGW Consulting to make
management decisions while potentially shielding it from liability for any actions. And
finally, we have concerns about transparency….

We are not here to impugn. We're not here to accuse. We are here to pose a question to the
applicants and of the Health Services Council, which is this: Is there a benefit to the
nursing homes and their residents as a result of these services being offered through
extended contracting relationships with related entities? Is there a benefit that is
conferred? And we're commenting on this transaction because we believe that this is a
question that you, the Health Services Council, should have the benefit of being able to
answer before rendering a decision. And further, we recommend that you consider
imposing conditions to ensure that the assets of these nursing homes are protected, and we
strongly recommend that you consider requiring that the fees being paid through this --
these agreements are set at cost, as they would be if this were a single entity.

   the proposed owners, operators or directors of the health care facility.

VI. CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL

The Council recommends that approval of the instant applications shall be subject to the
following conditions:

       1. that the total project cost of Coventry Operations RI LLC [Mayflower
          Healthcare LLC], Greenville Operations RI LLC [Mayflower Healthcare LLC]
          and Warren Operations RI LLC [Mayflower Healthcare LLC] shall not exceed
          $500,000.00. There is no financing for the proposed transaction.

       2. that the Applicants implement the projects as approved;

       3. that within two years the Applicants attain and maintain an accreditation from
          a nationally recognized accrediting agency;

       4. that the Applicants shall conduct national criminal background checks on their
          employees prior to employment;

       5. that data, including but not limited to, finances, utilization and demographic
          resident information, be furnished to the state agency, upon request;

       6. that the Applicants will provide services to all residents without discrimination
          including payment source and ability to pay; and

       7. that the Applicants will work in good faith with residents to establish an
          alternative payor source when another payor source is no longer available.
          Further that the Applicants will not withhold or discontinue care or discharge
          the resident while an application for an alternative payor source (such as
          Medicaid) is pending, and for Medicaid residents, maintain compliance with
          state regulation, 210-RICR-50-00-7, “Involuntary Discharge from a Long-
          Term Care Facility.”
