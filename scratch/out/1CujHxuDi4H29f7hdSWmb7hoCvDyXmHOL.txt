Dear Menachem Yifat:

In accordance with RI Gen. Laws 23-17-14.4 (d), I am varying from the recommendation of the
Council and denying this application for the reasons set forth below.

At its meeting of June 22, 2021, the Health Services Council (“Council”) recommended that the
application of Ballou Nursing & Rehab LLC for a Change in Effective Control (“CEC”) of BallouLife
Communities, [License # LTC00525], a licensed 43-bed nursing facility located at 60 Mendon Road,
Woonsocket, RI 02895, be approved, subject to the standard conditions of approval along with two
additional conditions of approval.

The two additional conditions were (1) that the Rhode Island Department of Health (“RIDOH”)
receive a positive approval of the application by the Attorney General's office, and (2) that RIDOH
receive an attestation from the applicant indicating that there are no active reports from the State
of New York concerning the two facilities run by the applicant in that state (attestation of the
good standing in NY).

By way of background, the Executive Summary of the application resubmitted on May 3, 2021, states
that “Mr. Yifat has over 12 years of experience in the health care industry and is the principal
managing member at Onyx Healthcare Group, LLC (“Onyx”),” Further, “Onyx is an affiliated entity
of the anticipated management company for the New Operator, Onyx Healthcare at Ballou, LLC.”
Further, Under Mr. Yifat’s leadership, Onyx has a proven track record in providing high quality care
in the skilled nursing and assisted living setting. Specifically, Onyx currently operates Foltsbrook
Center Nursing and Rehabilitation Center (163 bed skilled nursing facility in Herkimer, NY) and
Foltsbrook Senior Living (80 bed assisted living facility in Herkimer, NY.” Further, “Mr. Yifat
maintains an ownership interest in both of those facilities as well. Under Mr. Yifat’s leadership, the
facilities improved the quality of care, and became financially stable.”

After the June 22, 2021 meeting of the Council, RIDOH learned that a Stipulation and Order had been
issued by the Department of Health of the State of New York, NH 19-023 in the matter of FoltsCare,

LLC as operator of Foltsbrook Center for Nursing and Rehabilitation in Herkimer, NY (“NYS
Stipulation and Order,” copy attached). Furthermore, your legal counsel sent a letter to RIDOH dated.
June 29, 2021 that included a letter from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (“CMS”)
dated August 13, 2019 that noted, among other things, that the subject of the Stipulation and Order
resulted in a civil monetary penalty of $84,903 and that a NATCEP Prohibition was imposed for

The NYS Stipulation and Order directly contradicts the information set forth in the CEC application
under review. Specifically, Question 20 (A) of the Change in Effective Control application requires
the applicant to:

“Please identify and describe all instances involving the applicant and/or its affiliates and
the status or disposition of each of the following within the past 3 years:

A. — Citations, enforcement actions, violations, charges, investigations, or similar types of
actions involving the applicant and/or its affiliates (including but not limited to actions
brought forward by any governmental agency, accrediting agency, or similar type of an
agency.)

--to which the applicant stated “None.” RIDOH considers this to be a material misrepresentation of
fact in your application. You are reminded that you attested to the accuracy of the information in the
application when you signed it on January 15, 2021, stating “I hereby certify that the information
contained in this application is complete, accurate and true.”

Your June 22, 2021 failure to disclose to the Council the NYS Stipulation and Order, a substantial
enforcement action that included sanctions extending into 2021, is considered by RIDOH to be
misleading by omission. In the context of that proceeding it is my opinion that this constitutes a
material misrepresentation to the members of the Council about the past status of the facility’s license.

During the June 22, 2021 meeting of the Council you were expressly asked “[C]an you report, are
those two facilities in good standing?” You stated in response: “Yes, they’re in good standing.
Obviously cause of COVID, there haven’t been surveys in probably over two years; however, our —
all our infection control surveys have been zero deficiencies. So, we’ve had a good record.” This
does not in fact, appear to be the case:

The June 29, 2021 letter from your legal counsel also included a letter dated June 22, 2021 from Cindy
S. Pullano of the New York State Department of Health to Mark Scalise who is the Administrator of
Foltsbrook that included, among other things, the findings of a survey which took place on or about
June 3, 2021 wherein “isolated deficiencies that constitute actual harm that is Immediate
Jeopardy whereby significant corrections were required (J) and Substandard Quality of Care
was identified on 06/03/2021.” (emphasis not added). The New York state letter noted that “The
facility failed to present burns caused by foods and drinks microwaved to unsafe temperatures which
resulted in Resident #93 sustaining burns, including a second degree burn to their abdomen, from
food/beverages on multiple occasions after the food/beverages were microwaved either by the
resident or by staff members” (emphasis added). Furthermore, the CMS letter dated June 22, 2021
noted as part of a Directed Plan of Correction the facility must, among other things,” (c)onvene the
facility’ s/organization’s Quality Assurance (QA) Committee to examine the deficiencies cited under:

The results of the extended recertification survey conducted by the New York State Department of
Health, specifically the reports of Immediate Jeopardy and Substandard Quality of Care, were known
to you or should have been known to you at the time your application was considered by the Council.
Indeed, a key component of your application for Change in Effective Control was your representation
of competence and experience in the operation of the Foltsbrook Center Nursing and Rehabilitation
Center.

Your failure to inform the Council about the results of the New York Department of Health survey of
June 3-9, 2021, whether intentional or for lack of familiarity about the status of that facility, raises
serious questions with regard to your application. RIDOH and the Council rely on the information
represented by the applicant’s testimony and application to make our determinations. The statutory
review criteria for a Change in Effective Control review includes the “character, commitment,
competence, and standing in the community” and “the extent to which the facility ... will continue to
provide safe and adequate treatment.” (RI Gen. Laws 23-17-14.3)

In consideration of the statutory criteria and the clear and unambiguous misrepresentations made to
the Council and RIDOH, your application for a change in effective control is denied.
