I am writing in response to the April 17, 2026 letter from the Rhode Island Office of the
Attorney General (“RIAG”) in connection with the above referenced CEC Application (the

the “Applicants”). The above-referenced CEC Applications are on the agenda for tomorrow’s
meeting. We will be present with a brief PowerPoint presentation, are available to answer any
questions you may have and are asking that you recommend approval of the Applications on the
grounds that the Applicants meet all statutory review criteria.

As you will recall, on January 31, 2025, RIDOH approved the CEC applications for the
operations of AdviniaCare Pawtucket Pleasant Rehab Center, LLC (“AdviniaCare Pawtucket”)
and AdviniaCare Providence Dodge Rehab Center, LLC (“AdviniaCare Providence”)– both
affiliated entities of the Applicants. In addition, commencing in February, 2023, affiliated
entities also provided management services to the two nursing facilities pursuant to a RIDOH-
approved management agreement. Since their management of those facilities, the annual survey
outcomes significantly improved, the operators prioritized decreasing turnover in key positions,
were able to eliminate all agency staff and worked collaboratively with the unions in those
facilities, as well as infusing capital into the facilities. After CEC approval in 2025, the operators
continued to ensure quality care to all residents.

   1. The New Operator Will Maintain Control Over Management of the Facility and the
      Applicants Provided Ample Information Regarding the Corporate Manager,
      AdviniaCare, LLC, and Will Pay a Fair Market Corporate Management Fee.

The Letter faults the corporate manager of the Applicants, AdviniaCare, for the corporate duties
listed in the operating agreements provided to RIDOH as part of the CEC Applications. RIAG
made the same comments regarding the same corporate structure for the Applicants in the five
Athena facilities for which CEC approval was granted in December 2025. Such an argument is
misplaced as the Applicants have provided fulsome information regarding their corporate
manager. As set forth in the applications, the New Operator will be the licensed operator and will
be the responsible entity for operations. Moreover, the Applicants have provided all necessary
information about their corporate manager, AdviniaCare. For example, the Applicants have
provided the operating agreement of AdviniaCare (see Tab 26) and the organizational chart of
AdviniaCare (see Tab 15). There is no further information to provide. The Applicants have

Additionally, the corporate management fee is consistent with the previously approved corporate

   2. The Rent and Lease Agreement Provisions Are Fair Market Value, Industry
      Standard, and Have Been Fully Disclosed

RIAG’s contentions related to the lease agreement ignore the disclosures in the CEC
Applications and industry standards. First, the RIAG is concerned regarding whether the “rent
[is] intended to cover the mortgage – and if not, what does the rent go toward, and who pays the
mortgage.” As set forth in Appendix A and herein, the Applicant plans to increase operational
and financial stability. The rent amounts reflect fair market value based on appraisals. The
Applicants have disclosed the rent payments, the draft leases, and the financial projections. The
New Operator is responsible for the fair market value rent and the New Real Estate Owner is
responsible for the mortgage payments.

Second, the RIAG’s concern that the “lease has been modified [via a rider], but the terms of the
rider have not been included in the application” is misplaced. The rider is attached to the lease in
the application at Tab 24 (page 34 of the lease, page 251 of the PDF of the CEC Application).
The rider simply clarifies that the lease shall commence on a date to be determined as the closing
has not yet occurred.

Third, the RIAG appears confused about the term of the lease. As set forth in Article III of the
lease at Tab 24, the lease has a ten year term.

Finally, the RIAG’s contention that the lease agreement may “affect patient care” is nonsensical.
There is no provision with such language in the lease agreements. The standard indemnification
provision in the lease agreements states:

               25.1 Lessee agrees to protect, indemnify and save harmless the
               Lessor from and against any and all claims, demands and causes of
               action of any nature whatsoever asserted against or incurred by
               such parties on account of: (i) any failure on the part of Lessee
               during the term of this Lease to perform or comply with any of the
               terms of this Lease; or (ii) injury to or death of persons or loss of
               or damage to property, occurring on the Demised Premises or any
               adjoining sidewalks, streets or ways or in any manner growing out
               of or connected with the use or occupation of the Demised
               Premises or the condition thereof, or the use of any existing or
               future sewer system, or the use of any adjoining sidewalks, streets
               or ways occurring during the term of this Lease. Lessee further
               agrees to pay any reasonable attorneys’ fees and expenses incident
               to the defense by such parties of any such claims, demands or
               causes of action.

See Article XXV (Indemnification) (Tab 24). This provision is not only standard in lease
agreements, but it also does not shield the operator from any liability. It, instead, results in the
indemnification of the New Real Estate Owner by the New Operator for claims related to a
failure to comply with the terms of the lease and any injury, death, or other damages on or
related to the premises. The provision, therefore, requires that the New Operator would be
required to pay reasonable attorneys’ fees and expenses, as well as any damages related to such

    3. The Related Entity Subcontracts are Reasonable

As the RIAG previously raised with respect to the other AdviniaCare applications, it once again
questions the Applicants’ relationships with its disclosed related entity contractors – Curis
Services LLC (“Curis”), Global Distributors USA, LLC, Kesser Group, LLC, and AdviniaCare,
LLC. These relationships are substantively identical to the prior AdviniaCare applicants’
contracts with the related entities. The Curis contract sets forth the payment rate (1% of Revenue
with a floor of $11,000 per month). Similarly, the operating agreement for the Operations
Applicant sets forth the corporate management fee as set forth above.1 All terms with these
entities are at fair market value and the Applicants have provided all information related to these
entities.
