To the Health Services Council:

On behalf of the Office of the Health Care Advocate at the Rhode Island Office of the Attorney
General (the “Office”), we submit this letter in response to Change in Effective Control Application
(2026 CEC Application) for newly-formed operating and real estate entities affiliated with
AdviniaCare, LLC. This transaction will result in the acquisition of Elderwood of Scallop Shell at
Wakefield, an 80-bed skilled nursing facility in South Kingstown, Rhode Island by entities associated
with AdviniaCare LLC.

This Office has commented previously on the use of related entities in skilled nursing transactions,
both generally and as to prior AdviniaCare transactions. These facilities provide lifesaving care to some
of Rhode Island’s most vulnerable residents. As you know, skilled nursing facilities operate with
limited financial resources and are primarily funded by public insurance, e.g. Medicaid and Medicare. 1
If resources are not used prudently in the service of resident care, quality of care is likely to suffer.

Given AdviniaCare’s extensive use of related entities, the Office recommends that the Health Services
Council require additional information from the applicants, including review of contract terms,
payments, and allocation of responsibility between the licensed entities and their parent companies, in
order to better understand the financial impact of this transaction on the facilities and potential
impacts on quality of care for residents in these facilities.

Background

Related entities are companies under common ownership with a nursing home administrator with
whom the nursing home contracts. The risk to the nursing home is that, if related entities are charging
above-market rates for the contracted services, the precious resources of these facilities will be diverted
away from patient care as profit. Transactions involving related entities may be linked to asset shielding
(where single-use LLCs serve to insulate parent companies from liability) and profit tunnelling (where
LLCs under common ownership migrate profits away from patient care through inflated fees). The
use of related entities may also lead to distorted financial reporting to Medicare and Medicaid,
permitting nursing home operators to list expenses as “costs” billed through arms-length transactions,
when they are in fact profits shifted to common owners.

A working paper published by the National Bureau of Economic Research suggests 68% of profits in
the skilled nursing care industry were hidden through related entity transactions in 2019. 2 Price
increases were especially prevalent in real estate and management services, with costs increasing by
42.4% and 24.6%, respectively, when the operator transacted with a related entity.

Key Issues and Recommendations

To ensure a diligent review of the application and to guard against potential risks to patient care created
by the transacting parties’ relationships with related entities, the Office recommends that the Health
Services Council review for and address the following issues raised by the application:

    1. Who Retains Effective Control Over Management of the Facilities?

        There is genuine ambiguity as to whether effective control—in terms of duties, responsibility,
        and liability—lies with Scallop Shell Operator or AdviniaCare, LLC. The Operating
        Agreement of Scallop Shell Operator indicates that “the business and affairs of [Scallop Shell
        Operator] shall be managed under the sole discretion and control of the Managers,” i.e.
        AdviniaCare LLC, which has “the sole and exclusive right to manage the day-to-day operations
        of the Company.” See 2026 CEC Application, Tab 26, Operating Agreement for AdviniaCare
        Scallop Shell LLC, Article 5.1, Management by Managers.

        Under the terms of the Operating Agreement, AdviniaCare LLC has authority to:

        (i)      Supervise operations and management
        (ii)     Authorize acquisitions, leases, and purchase options
        (iii)    Acquire insurance for the Company
        (iv)     Determine appropriate reserves for future expenses
        (v)      Determine operating and capital expenditures, budgets, and annual marketing plans
        (vi)     Borrow money, enter into financing / refinancing transactions
        (vii)    Acquire personal, tangible, and intangible property
        (viii)   Contract on behalf of the Company with non-affiliates
        (ix)     Request additional capital contributions from members
        (x)      Authorize additional interests and admit new members
        (xi)     Obtain licenses, approvals, and permits
        (xii)    Make decisions on behalf of the company relating to compliance with government
                 agencies or programs and/or licensure requirements
        (xiii)   Hire and fire all employees, officers, and directors
        (xiv)    Execute contracts 4

        “without limitation” to enter into management or consultant contracts on behalf of Scallop
        Shell Operator. The Operating Agreement thus appears to grant AdviniaCare LLC broad

    4. What is the Basis for the 5% Management Fee?

Conclusion

The Office recommends that the Health Services Council make a full inquiry as to these and any other
relevant terms prior to approval of this CEC application. We hope that future reviews can incorporate
a standing review of related entities held by the applicant and any plans to utilize related entities in the
administration of Rhode Island facilities following CEC approval.

Please let us know if there are any questions we can answer to assist in your review.
