To the Health Services Council:

On behalf of the Office of the Health Care Advocate at the Rhode Island Office of the Attorney
General, we submit this letter in response to the Change in Effective Control (CEC) applications filed
by Mayflower Healthcare LLC for the acquisition of three Rhode Island nursing homes: Respiratory
and Rehab Center of Rhode Island [LTC00789], Greenville Skilled Nursing and Rehabilitation
[LTC00792], and Warren Skilled Nursing and Rehabilitation [LTC00796].

Nursing homes play a critical role in Rhode Island’s health care ecosystem. As you know, skilled
nursing facilities operate with limited financial resources and are primarily funded by public insurance,
e.g. Medicaid and Medicare. The health and well-being of Rhode Island residents in skilled nursing
facilities is often directly tethered to the financial health of these facilities.

The Office of the Attorney General submits this comment letter with respect to certain disclosures
made in these CEC applications as to Mayflower Healthcare LLC’s use of “related entities.” We raise
these questions to the Health Services Council to recommend further investigation and consideration
of related entities with respect to all nursing home CEC applications, including the applications at
hand, and to recommend instituting safeguards to ensure that the resources of our nursing homes are
protected for the benefit of their residents.

Background: Related Entities

Related entities are companies under common ownership or control with whom the nursing home
contracts. While nursing home operators are permitted to be reimbursed for expenses incurred by
related entities, these expenses must typically be billed at cost or, in certain circumstances, at fair
market rates. 42 C.F.R. § 413.17.

A key concern – particularly if related entities are undisclosed – is that they may be used to siphon
profits away from patient care, leaving regulators in the dark as to potential risks to health and safety. 1
A working paper published by the National Bureau of Economic Research suggests 68% of profits in
the skilled nursing care industry were hidden through related entity transactions in 2019. 2 Price
increases were especially prevalent in real estate and management services, with costs increasing by
42.4% and 24.6%, respectively, when the operator transacted with a related entity. If these profits had
been spent on registered nurses, staffing could have increased by 35.7%.

Lack of disclosure also compromises the accuracy of operators’ cost reporting to Medicare and
Medicaid and may lead to distorted financial reporting, permitting nursing home operators to list these
expenses as “costs” billed through arms-length transactions, when they are in fact profits shifted to
common owners of related entities. 3 These transactions have a direct bearing on facility resources,
thereby implicating patient care.

Mayflower Healthcare LLC CEC Applications

The CEC applications before the Health Services Council seeks a change in effective control for three
newly-formed entities—Coventry Operations RI LLC, Greenville Operations RI LLC, and Warren
Operations RI LLC—operating under common ownership by Mayflower Healthcare LLC, a newly-
formed LLC owned by Zev Schwartz. The applications disclose two key contractual relationships: a
consulting relationship with RGW Consulting LLC and a leasing arrangement with Gentech RGW
LLC. The primary landlord, OHI Asset (CT) Lender LLC, currently leases to these nursing homes
and will continue to do so.

The applications do not name these contractual partners as related entities of the proposed operators,

RGW Consulting LLC

RGW Consulting LLC is a domestic LLC that was formed on October 23, 2023. It is not disclosed
as a subsidiary, parent, or sister entity in response to Question 15 or included in the Post Transaction
Organizational Chart at Tab 15. However, the Office of the Attorney General understands that RGW
Consulting LLC may be a related entity of the new operators and mutually owned by Zev Schwartz.
Zev Schwartz – the sole member of Mayflower LLC – is listed as both the “manager” of the operating
LLCs and the “sole member” of RGW Consulting LLC and is the authorized signer for both entities.
See Tab 1 – Consulting Agreements, Signature Page.

According to the consulting agreements attached to this application, Mayflower plans to engage RGW
Consulting LLC (“Consultant”) as its consulting firm for each of the three LLC operators and will pay
a consulting fee of 5% of all “project income” (defined as all revenue from each operating LLC, with
certain exclusions). The agreements include a stipulation that this 5% fee is “fair market value” for
services rendered. See Tab 1 – Consulting Agreements, Article I. Definitions, Section 1.5; Article VI,
Section 6.1. The application does not offer any further justification as to whether and to what extent
such a percentage-based fee is reasonable or prudent.

Further, the indemnification provisions of the agreements raise concerns that the agreements favor
the Consultant at the expense of the operating LLC, by providing for one-sided indemnification
provisions that would leave the operating LLCs liable for claims arising out of the Consultant's work,
or the work of their subcontractors. See Tab 1 – Consulting Agreements, Article II, Sections 2.2, 2.5.
None of the provisions listed above appear to include reciprocal provisions to protect the operating
LLCs. Rather, these provisions appear to generally accrue benefit to RGW Consulting LLC at the
expense of the operator LLCs, insulating the latter’s assets.

We also note—notwithstanding the representation in Question 8 that the facility will not be under a
management agreement—that the agreements appear to grant RGW Consulting LLC effective control
over a wide range of operational responsibilities that go to the core of the management of a nursing
home, including:

   •   Administration of a resident relations program;
   •   Advising on employment decisions;
   •   Locating and recommending a licensed nursing home administrator (including authority to
       recommend interim administrators / directors of nursing employed by the Consultant);
   •   Recommending service contracts, including companies and affiliates of the Consultant

Gentech RGW RI LLC

The Office of the Attorney General also raises the possibility of a relationship between the new
operators and Gentech RGW RI, LLC. Gentech RGW RI LLC is newly formed on October 13, 2023.
It is not disclosed as a subsidiary, parent, or sister entity in response to Question 15 or included in the
Post Transaction Organizational Chart at Tab 15.

RGW Consulting LLC and Gentech RGW RI, LLC were both formed in October 2023, share a
resident agent and authorized signers with the operator LLCs, and list “G8 Financial” as the contact
person in their annual reports filed with the Rhode Island Secretary of State. The annual reports of
both entities are attached as Exhibit A. As such, we raise the question as to whether Gentech RGW
LLC is affiliated with RGW Consulting LLC. Gentech RGW RI LLC also appears to be directly
affiliated with the operator LLCs, serving as their guarantor. See Tab 14 – Operations Transfer
Agreements. Appendix C, Question 3 requires the operator LLCs are required to show proof of their
ability to operate for thirty days. Mayflower Healthcare LLC provided a letter of assurance from
Gentech’s certified public accountants, which states Gentech’s affiliates have been in the nursing
home industry for many years and have the necessary experience and track record to complete the
transaction and operate the nursing facility.”

Recommendations

We encourage the Health Services Council to require additional information from the operating
LLCs prior to approving the CEC application to determine if this application involves related
entities, including specific inquiry as to the following issues:

    •   The nature of the relationship between Mayflower Healthcare LLC, Zev Schwartz, RGW
        Consulting LLC, and Gentech RGW LLC, including whether Zev Schwartz has an
        ownership interest in or is otherwise affiliated with RGW Consulting LLC and/or Gentech
        RGW RI LLC.

    •   Information about RGW Consulting LLC and Gentech RGW LLC's ultimate parent
        companies and affiliates, including G8 Financial. For Gentech RGW RI LLC, RGW
        Consulting LLC, and G8 Financial, we recommend requesting, to the extent not already
        provided, members and/or managers associated with these entities, parent entities, and
        copies of certificates of organization and operating agreements for each entity.

    •   The applicant LLCs plan to sublease the Leased Properties from Gentech RGW RI LLC,
        which in turn leases the properties from OHI Asset (CT) Lender, LLC. We recommend that
        the Health Services Council inquire further into the purpose and value to the nursing homes
        of this tiered lease arrangement.

    •   Further information about the proposed benefit of the consulting services provided by
        RGW Consulting LLC, specifying the value that the operating LLCs will expect gain under
        those agreements and clarifying why those functions are not able to be performed by the
        operating LLCs at cost.

    •   Information explaining why RGW Consulting LLC is not a “management company” subject
        to disclosure under 216 R.I. Code R. 40-10-1.7.2. 6

We encourage the Health Services Council to require this additional information from the applicants
and consider the use of and payment to related entities by the applicants. Following such an inquiry,
if the Health Services Council determines that a recommendation in favor of this transaction is
proper, we recommend that the Health Services Council and the Rhode Island Department of
Health consider imposing conditions on the transaction requiring operators to: (1) disclose and
identify any related entities contracted with the applicants, now and in the future; (2) require
reporting of all agreements between the operator and any mutually-owned or controlled related
entities, including security agreements, and any subsequent changes to those agreements; and (3)
