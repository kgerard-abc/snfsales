Pursuant to R.|. Gen. Laws § 23-17.9-1 et seq. and the Rules and Regulations
promulgated thereunder, the Rhode Island Department of Health (“RIDOH"),
has investigated this complaint regarding allegations of unprofessional conduct
in violation of R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-17.9-1 ef seq. against ROSERLI RICHARDSON
PAULINO (“Respondent”), and after review and consideration by RIDOH with
respect to the allegations of unprofessional conduct of Respondent, the
following are and shall constitute:

FINDINGS OF FACT

1. Respondent is a Nursing Assistant licensed to practice in Rhode Island
under Nursing Assistant License NA 51655.

2. That from July 10, 2023, up to and including August 2, 2024, Respondént was
employed by several staffing agencies and assigned to provide ¢are to
patients in their homes. }

3. That on or about August 1, 2024, Respondent was scheduled to work at
the home of a patient from 3:15 p.m. to 8:15 p.m. Respondent arrived at
the home at 3:15 p.m. She left the home at 5:45 p.m. but did not clock
out. Respondent returned to the home at 8:15 but did not get out of her
car. She electronically reported that she had completed tasks, checked
off her own signature, and forged the patient's signature, thus having
abandoned the patient who was in need of her care.

4. That on or about August 2, 2024, the same patient described above told
Respondent that he would not be home for her scheduled visit and that
there was no need for her to come to his home. However, on that same
day, Respondent drove to the patient's home, clocked in at 3:15 and left
without entering the home. Respondent returned to the area at 8:15
p.m., clocked herself out, forged the patient’s signature, and left, again
without entering the home.

. From July 10, 2023, up to and including August 2, 2024, Respondent was
employed by more than one staffing agency and was assigned to work
at the homes of different patients in different location at the same time.
Respondent would clock in to one location, leave prior to the end of the
scheduled shift, and clock in at another location, thus abandoning
patients in need of her care.

. The conduct described in paragraphs three, four, and five of this Consent
Order constitutes conduct subject to disciplinary proceedings pursuant to
R. |. Gen. Laws §23-17.9-1 et seq. and the Rules and Regulations
promulgated thereunder.
