i. Introduction

The above-entitled matter came before the Board of Nurse Registration and Nursing
Education (“Board”) pursuant to a Notice of Charges and Administrative Hearing (“Notice”)
issued on December 19, 2017 by the Board to Christine Wood-Grattan (“Respondent”). The
Respondent holds a license (“License”) as a registered nurse in the State of Rhode Island pursuant
to R.L Gen. Laws § 5-34-1 ef seq. A hearing was scheduled for January 8, 2018. The Board was
represented by counsel and the Respondent was pro se. The parties rested on the record.

i. Jurisdiction

The Board has jurisdiction over this matter pursuant to R.I. Gen. Laws § 5-34-1 ef seq.,
Rules and Regulations for the Licensing of Nurses/and Standards for the Approval of Basic
Nursing Education Programs, and Rules and Regulations of the Department of Health Regarding

Practices and Procedures Before the Department of Health.
I. Issue

Whether the Respondent violated R.I. Gen. Laws § 5-34-24(6)(v) by engaging in
unprofessional conduct! and if so, what should be the sanction.
IV. Material Facts and Testimony

Margaret Clifton, Nursing Board Administrator, testified that Rhode Island is part of the
nursing license compact state,” and that discipline had been imposed on the Respondent in New
Hampshire. She testified that New Hampshire made five (5) findings of unprofessional conduct
by the Respondent. She testified that the findings were 1) use of air in syringe as means to expel
full dose of medication into a PICC line; 2) while verbalizing competence, found a lack of
significant knowledge in the use of lab unit equipment; 3) after preceptor review of unit policy on
amount of blood draw waste (3-5 mls), Respondent drew a lab sample and wasted 10 ml of blood;
4) medication preparation of incorrect dose and syringe not labeled; and 5) undocumented narcotic
waste. See Department’s Exhibit One (1) (New Hampshire decision dated June 19, 2017). She
testified that those actions all represented unprofessional conduct within the State of Rhode Island.

Vv. Discussion

The Board found that while the Respondent tried to explain the findings of the New

Hampshire decision, she admitted to the unprofessional conduct that had been found by New
Hampshire (findings one (1) through four (4)) such as the air in syringe while on the PICC line,
the misuse of the equipment, and not Jabeling a syringe. The Board also found that the Respondent
provided contradictory and inconsistent explanations. For example, the 7 year old patient was
either a pediatric patient or an adult patient and in the Respondent’s explanations of her treatment
of him, she changed his patient status depending on the type of treatment (e.g. the drawing of blood
as opposed to the administration of pain medication).

Based on the findings by New Hampshire and the evidence at hearing, the Board found
that the Respondent engaged in unprofessional conduct and violated R.L Gen. Laws § 5-34-
24(6)(v) (willfully disregarding standards of nursing practice and failing to maintain standards of
nursing profession).
VI. Conclusion

After hearing and based on the forgoing, the Board made the following order:

The Respondent’s License is suspended until June 19, 2018.
