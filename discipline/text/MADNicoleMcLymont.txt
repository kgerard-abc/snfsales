RHODE ISLAND DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH
DIVISION OF HEALTHCARE QUALITY AND SAFETY
CENTER FOR PROFESSIONAL BOARDS AND LICENSING

IN THE MATTER OF: NICOLE A. MCLYMONT

LICENSEE NO.: MAD03477
COMPLAINT ID #: C25-0964

CONSENT ORDER FOR REPRIMAND

In accordance with R.I. Gen. Laws §§ 23-17.9-1-23-17.9-14 (the “Registration and Duty
of Care of Nursing Assistants” or “Nursing Assistant Statute”) and the Rules and Regulations
promulgated thereunder, 216-RI ADC T. 216, Ch. 40, Subch.05, Pt. 22 (the “Nursing Assistant
/Medication Aide Regulations”), the Rhode Island Department of Health (“RIDOH”) has
investigated this complaint #C25-0964 (the “Complaint”), charging NICOLE MCLYMONT
(“Respondent”) with unprofessional conduct.

Pursuant to Section 14.15 of the Rules and Regulations Pertaining to the Practices and
Procedures before the Rhode Island Department of Health, 216-RICR-10-05-4.15, RIDOH and
Respondent, (together, the “parties”), agree to dispose of the above-captioned matter by entering
into this Consent Order in lieu of an administrative hearing. By consent of the parties, this Consent
Order is enforceable as a final order of RIDOH.

Respondent admits that RIDOH has jurisdiction over this matter and agrees to waive
further procedural steps and any right to appeal this Consent Order. Respondent further agrees that
RIDOH may make the Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law set forth below.

FINDINGS OF FACT

1. Respondent is a Medication Aide, licensed to practice in Rhode Island under Medication
Aide License MAD03477 (the “License”).

2. On or about July 19, 2025, Respondent posted a video to social media that included an
image of a medication card with the name of a resident of an assisted living facility where
Respondent formerly worked as a Medication Aide.

3. Respondent has stated that she was “making a video to show the life” of a certified
medical technician, and that when she realized her posted video included an image of a
medication card with a resident’s name, she immediately removed and deleted her post.
CONCLUSIONS OF LAW

4. In Rhode Island, nursing assistants and medication aides are expected to respect patients’
rights, which includes the right of patients to have their personal health information kept
confidential and secure. This expectation is reflected in the requirements pertaining to
State-licensed nursing assistant training programs.'

5. The conduct described in Paragraphs 2-3 of this Consent Order constitutes unprofessional
conduct pursuant to the following provisions:

a. Rhode Island Gen. Laws § 23-17.9-8 (2)(“the nursing assistant has violated . . .
the rules enacted in accordance with this chapter”);

b. Section 22.6(A)(2) of the Nursing Assistant/Medication Aide Regulations, 216-
RICR-40-05-22 (“the nursing assistant or medication aide has engaged in
unprofessional conduct including, but not limited to, departure from, or failure to
conform to, the standards of acceptable and prevailing practice).

Whereas Respondent wishes to avoid a disciplinary proceeding on this matter and agrees
to voluntarily enter into this Consent Order in order to resolve the Complaint against her License,
and whereas RIDOH finds the within disposition sufficiently protective of the public health, safety,
and welfare, and for good cause shown, RIDOH and Respondent hereby agree to the following

disposition of the Complaint.
